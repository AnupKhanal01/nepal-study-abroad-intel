# Australia — student visa/immigration checklist

> **last_verified:** 2026-09-13 (general knowledge current to Jan 2026 — re-verify TO VERIFY
> items against the Department of Home Affairs; Australian student-visa policy has changed
> frequently in recent years, including for Nepal specifically.)

- **Region:** Australia/Oceania
- **Visa/permit name:** **Student visa (subclass 500)**
- **Issuing authority:** Department of Home Affairs
- **Relevant for Nepal applicants because:** Home Affairs applies a country/institution-based
  **risk framework** to student visa assessment; Nepal has at times been associated with higher
  refusal rates and closer scrutiny for certain provider/course combinations — TO VERIFY current
  risk settings and whether any Ministerial Direction currently affects processing priority for
  Nepal-origin applications (Home Affairs has issued Ministerial Directions re-ordering visa
  processing by provider risk level, which indirectly affects Nepali applicants heavily).

## Entry / academic requirements
- Institution-set. Visa assessment itself centers on the **Genuine Student (GS) requirement**
  (which replaced the former Genuine Temporary Entrant test) — the applicant must demonstrate
  genuine intent to study and temporarily stay, not a route to permanent migration.

## English language requirement
- Institution-set (commonly IELTS ~6.0–6.5 for degree programs) — TO VERIFY per course; some
  providers require higher scores for high-risk-country applicants or offer English bridging
  packages instead.

## Financial / bank requirement
- Must demonstrate financial capacity to cover tuition, travel, and an annual living-cost
  amount set by Home Affairs (revised roughly yearly) — exact current figure: TO VERIFY.
- Funds evidence can be via personal/family savings, a genuine loan, or a scholarship —
  documentation standards are strict for applicants from higher-scrutiny countries.

## Health insurance
- **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** is mandatory for the visa duration — cost: TO VERIFY
  (varies by provider and duration/family coverage).

## Visa/permit process
- Requires a **Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)** from the institution before applying; the GS
  requirement includes a written statement addressing course/career relevance.
- Processing time and visa fee: TO VERIFY — Home Affairs publishes indicative processing times
  that vary significantly by risk assessment.

## Work rights while studying
- Capped hours during term (a fortnightly limit was reinstated after a pandemic-era removal;
  the exact cap has been adjusted more than once) — TO VERIFY current cap; generally unlimited
  during scheduled course breaks.

## Post-study work / stay-back route
- **Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485)** — duration depends on qualification level and, for
  some fields, "designated area"/regional study bonuses; eligibility and durations have been
  tightened in recent policy rounds — TO VERIFY current terms.

## Dependents
- Rules on bringing dependents (and whether dependents can work) have been tightened and
  reversed more than once in recent years, and can depend on the course level — TO VERIFY
  current policy before treating any agency claim as reliable.

## Cost of living reference
- Home Affairs publishes its own annual living-cost figure for visa financial-capacity
  purposes — prefer that over an agency's estimate once verified.

## Notes specific to Jan/Apr 2027 intake
- Australia does not use a strict Jan/Apr academic calendar the way UK does — most universities
  have **Semester 1 (Feb/Mar)** and **Semester 2 (Jul)** intakes, sometimes with additional
  trimester intakes. Confirm what an agency's "Jan 2027" or "Apr 2027" claim actually maps to —
  it may refer to a pathway/foundation program start date rather than the main degree intake.
- Given the history of policy volatility (visa processing prioritization by provider risk,
  financial capacity amount, work-hour caps, dependent rules, PGWP-equivalent durations), treat
  this file as needing a fresh check before every intake cycle, not just periodically.

## Sources to check when verifying
- Department of Home Affairs (homeaffairs.gov.au / immi.homeaffairs.gov.au).
