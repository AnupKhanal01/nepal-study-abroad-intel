# Germany — student visa/immigration checklist

> **last_verified:** 2026-09-13 (general knowledge current to Jan 2026 — re-verify all TO VERIFY
> items, especially the blocked-account amount, which is revised roughly annually.)

- **Region:** Europe
- **Visa/permit name:** National (Type D) student visa on entry, then a residence permit for
  study purposes (Aufenthaltserlaubnis zum Studium) from the local Ausländerbehörde
- **Issuing authority:** German Federal Foreign Office (visa) / local Ausländerbehörde (residence
  permit); applications typically processed via the German Embassy/Consulate covering Nepal and
  often through **uni-assist** for document pre-checking.
- **Relevant for Nepal applicants because:** Confirm current visa jurisdiction (embassy in New
  Delhi has historically covered Nepal for some German consular services — verify).

## Entry / academic requirements
- Depends on program and whether it's a "Studienkolleg" (foundation year) pathway case; German
  university admission offices assess Nepali qualifications individually, often via uni-assist.

## English language requirement
- For English-taught programs: institution-set (commonly IELTS ~6.0–6.5).
- For German-taught programs: German language proof required (commonly a DSH or TestDaF
  threshold, or a Studienkolleg + German course pathway) — TO VERIFY per program.

## Financial / bank requirement
- **Blocked account (Sperrkonto)** is the standard proof-of-funds mechanism: a fixed monthly
  amount, set annually by the Federal Foreign Office/BAMF, must be deposited and is released in
  monthly instalments. Exact current amount: TO VERIFY — this figure changes yearly and has risen
  substantially in recent years.
- Alternatives: a formal scholarship, a financial guarantee (Verpflichtungserklärung) from a
  German resident, or a bank guarantee.

## Health insurance
- Mandatory statutory (or in some cases private) health insurance is required to enrol at a
  German university — cost: TO VERIFY (statutory student rates are set periodically).

## Visa/permit process
- Type D national visa application at the German mission, then in-country registration
  (Anmeldung) and residence permit application at the local Ausländerbehörde after arrival.
- Processing time and visa fee: TO VERIFY.

## Work rights while studying
- Non-EU students are generally capped at **140 full days or 280 half days per year** (or an
  equivalent ~20 hrs/week average) — confirm current limit, as EU-wide student-work rules have
  been discussed for revision.

## Post-study work / stay-back route
- Graduates can typically get an **18-month job-seeker residence permit** to search for
  qualification-matching work, convertible to a work/EU Blue Card permit once employed —
  TO VERIFY current duration.

## Dependents
- Possible in some cases (e.g. spouse) but subject to its own income/housing requirements —
  TO VERIFY, not commonly used by single students.

## Cost of living reference
- The blocked-account monthly amount is itself Germany's own benchmark cost-of-living figure for
  visa purposes — use it in preference to agency estimates once verified.

## Notes specific to Jan/Apr 2027 intake
- Most German public universities intake mainly in **October (winter)** and **April (summer)** —
  confirm the "April 2027" target aligns with the specific university's actual semester start,
  and check application deadlines (often several months ahead, especially via uni-assist).

## Sources to check when verifying
- Federal Foreign Office (Auswärtiges Amt) and DAAD (daad.de) for study/visa guidance.
