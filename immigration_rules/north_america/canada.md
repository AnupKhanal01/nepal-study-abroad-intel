# Canada — student visa/immigration checklist

> **last_verified:** 2026-09-13 (general knowledge current to Jan 2026 — Canadian study-permit
> policy has changed very frequently since 2024 (intake caps, PAL requirement, financial
> threshold, PGWP eligibility). Treat every figure here as provisional and re-verify against
> IRCC before every intake cycle — this is the single highest-churn file in this folder.)

- **Region:** North America
- **Visa/permit name:** **Study permit** (this is a permit, not technically a "visa," though a
  Temporary Resident Visa or eTA is also issued alongside it depending on the applicant's
  situation)
- **Issuing authority:** Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC)
- **Relevant for Nepal applicants because:** Nepal has been one of the larger source countries
  for Canadian study permit applications; IRCC's 2024+ national intake cap and **Provincial
  Attestation Letter (PAL)** requirement materially changed how many applications can even be
  submitted per institution/province — this is likely the single most important recent policy
  shift to track for agencies marketing Canada.

## Entry / academic requirements
- Requires a **Letter of Acceptance** from a **Designated Learning Institution (DLI)** — confirm
  the specific institution is currently DLI-listed, since not all colleges qualify, and DLI
  status/eligibility for the PGWP can differ by program (see below).

## English language requirement
- Institution-set (commonly IELTS ~6.0–6.5) — TO VERIFY per program. Separately, as of a 2024
  policy change, **PGWP eligibility itself now has a language-test requirement** for certain
  program types (see Post-study section) — do not conflate admission language requirements with
  the newer PGWP-specific language requirement.

## Financial / bank requirement
- Must show proof of funds covering tuition + living costs for the applicant (and any
  accompanying family); IRCC updated its minimum living-cost financial requirement in **January
  2024** to a substantially higher figure than before, indexed to Statistics Canada's low-income
  cutoff — exact current amount: TO VERIFY (this is exactly the kind of figure that goes stale
  fast; do not reuse a number seen in an old agency post).
- A **Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL)** from the study-destination province/territory is now
  generally required as part of the study permit application (introduced 2024) — confirm the
  institution/program actually has PAL allocation before treating an "acceptance" as
  study-permit-ready.

## Health insurance
- Not federally mandated as part of the study permit itself, but provincial health coverage
  eligibility for international students varies by province (some provinces provide it, others
  require private insurance) — TO VERIFY per destination province.

## Visa/permit process
- Apply online (or via a Student Direct Stream-equivalent process where applicable) with the
  Letter of Acceptance, PAL, proof of funds, and biometrics.
- Processing time and fee: TO VERIFY — has fluctuated with application-volume caps.

## Work rights while studying
- Off-campus work hour cap was removed during COVID, then **reinstated** at a set weekly cap
  (2024 policy) during term, with full-time allowed during scheduled breaks — TO VERIFY current
  cap.

## Post-study work / stay-back route
- **Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)** — eligibility has been significantly narrowed since
  2024: field-of-study/program-type restrictions apply especially for programs delivered via
  **public-private college partnerships** (curriculum licensing arrangements), and a **language
  test requirement** now applies to PGWP applications. Duration is generally tied to program
  length (up to a cap). TO VERIFY current eligibility rules in detail before treating any
  agency's "guaranteed PGWP" claim as accurate — this is one of the most commonly oversold
  points in Canada-focused recruitment marketing right now.

## Dependents
- Rules for accompanying spouse/common-law partner work permits were also tightened in
  2024 (previously broader eligibility, now generally restricted to spouses of students in
  specific program types, e.g. master's/doctoral or professional/vocational programs on a
  designated list) — TO VERIFY current eligibility before relaying any agency claim about
  bringing a spouse.

## Cost of living reference
- IRCC's own updated financial-requirement figure (see above) is the relevant benchmark for
  visa purposes — prefer it over an agency's cost-of-living estimate once verified.

## Notes specific to Jan/Apr 2027 intake
- Because of the national intake cap and PAL system, **not every institution/program has
  allocation for every intake** — an agency marketing a "Jan 2027" or "Apr 2027" Canada intake
  should be asked directly whether a PAL is actually available for that specific program before
  a student commits a deposit.
- This file should be re-checked essentially every recruitment cycle given how frequently IRCC
  has changed rules since 2024.

## Sources to check when verifying
