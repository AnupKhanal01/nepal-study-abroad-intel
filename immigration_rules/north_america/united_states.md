# United States — student visa/immigration checklist

> **last_verified:** 2026-09-13 (general knowledge current to Jan 2026 — re-verify TO VERIFY
> items against the US Department of State and USCIS/SEVP.)

- **Region:** North America
- **Visa/permit name:** **F-1** (academic study) — **M-1** exists for vocational study but is
  rarely the route agencies market to Nepali students.
- **Issuing authority:** Status/school-side administered by SEVP (part of ICE/DHS) via SEVIS;
  the visa itself issued by the US Department of State through the US Embassy in Kathmandu.
- **Relevant for Nepal applicants because:** F-1 visa interviews at the US Embassy in Kathmandu
  are a well-known bottleneck (appointment availability, refusal-rate scrutiny under INA
  §214(b) "immigrant intent" presumption) — this is often the actual constraint, more than
  academic admission itself.

## Entry / academic requirements
- Institution-set; the government requirement is simply that the school is SEVP-certified and
  issues a valid **Form I-20**.

## English language requirement
- Institution-set (commonly IELTS ~6.0–6.5 or TOEFL equivalent, or a conditional-admission
  English pathway) — TO VERIFY per program.

## Financial / bank requirement
- The I-20 states an estimated annual cost of attendance; the applicant/sponsor must show funds
  (often for at least the first year, sometimes the full program) via bank statements,
  sponsor affidavits, or loan approval letters — exact expected amount is program-specific
  (read off the I-20), not a fixed government figure.

## Health insurance
- Not federally mandated by immigration law, but virtually all US institutions **require**
  enrollment in a health insurance plan (their own or an approved equivalent) as a condition of
  registration — cost: TO VERIFY per institution.

## Visa/permit process
- SEVIS I-901 fee payment → DS-160 online application → visa interview at the US Embassy in
  Kathmandu → (if approved) F-1 visa stamped, entry up to 30 days before program start.
- Processing time is dominated by **visa interview appointment availability**, which fluctuates
  significantly — TO VERIFY current wait times at the Kathmandu embassy specifically.

## Work rights while studying
- **On-campus** work up to 20 hrs/week during term (full-time during breaks) is generally
  allowed without separate authorization.
- **Off-campus** work requires specific authorization: **CPT** (Curricular Practical Training,
  tied to curriculum) or **OPT** (Optional Practical Training, post-completion) — not open
  part-time work like some other countries.

## Post-study work / stay-back route
- **OPT**: typically 12 months post-completion; **STEM OPT extension** adds up to 24 additional
  months for qualifying STEM degrees from an E-Verify employer — TO VERIFY current rules, as
  STEM-designated-degree lists and extension conditions are periodically updated.

## Dependents
- **F-2** dependent visas exist for spouse/children; F-2 dependents generally **cannot work**
  (spouse may study part-time under some conditions) — TO VERIFY current restrictions.

## Cost of living reference
- Extremely location-dependent (a small-town state university vs. a major metro campus) —
  always capture the specific campus city, not a national average.

## Notes specific to Jan/Apr 2027 intake
- US universities are overwhelmingly **Fall (Aug/Sep)** intake-driven; a smaller number offer
  **Spring (Jan)** intake, and **almost none** run a true "April" intake — if an agency post
  markets an "April 2027" US start, confirm carefully what program/institution that actually
  refers to, as it is atypical for this country and worth double-checking for accuracy.
- Visa-interview slot availability at the Kathmandu embassy is often the real bottleneck for
  timing — factor this into any deadline/process-duration claim from an agency.

## Sources to check when verifying
- US Department of State (travel.state.gov) and Study in the States / SEVP (ice.gov/sevis).
