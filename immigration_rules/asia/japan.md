# Japan — student visa/immigration checklist

> **last_verified:** 2026-09-13 (general knowledge current to Jan 2026 — re-verify TO VERIFY
> items with Japan's Immigration Services Agency.)

- **Region:** Asia
- **Visa/permit name:** "Student" status of residence, entered via a **Certificate of
  Eligibility (COE)** and a matching visa stamp
- **Issuing authority:** Immigration Services Agency of Japan (ISA); COE issued by the regional
  immigration bureau on the accepting institution's application; visa stamped by the Japanese
  Embassy/Consulate.
- **Relevant for Nepal applicants because:** Japan has a large Nepali student population
  (notably in Japanese-language schools feeding into vocational/university pathways); document
  scrutiny for financial sponsors has historically been strict for Nepal-origin applicants —
  TO VERIFY current practice.

## Entry / academic requirements
- Institution/program-set; language schools often require completion of secondary education plus
  a minimum hours of prior Japanese study for some tracks.

## English language requirement
- Only relevant for English-taught degree programs (a minority) — institution-set, TO VERIFY.
- For Japanese-taught programs, an accepted Japanese proficiency level (often JLPT N2 or
  equivalent, or completion of a Japanese language school) is typically required instead.

## Financial / bank requirement
- Sponsor's bank balance certificate/tax/income documents required to support the COE
  application; the university/language school defines the exact evidentiary bar in practice —
  TO VERIFY typical amount expected.

## Health insurance
- Enrollment in Japan's National Health Insurance is generally required/expected upon
  registering residency — cost: TO VERIFY.

## Visa/permit process
- Two-stage: COE application (by the school, on the student's behalf) → visa application at the
  Japanese embassy/consulate using the COE. Processing time: TO VERIFY (COE stage often the
  longer step).

## Work rights while studying
- Permitted to work part-time only with a **"permission to engage in activity other than that
  permitted under the status of residence previously granted"** — typically capped at **28
  hours/week** (with different rules during long school breaks) — confirm current cap.

## Post-study work / stay-back route
- Graduates can apply to change status to a **"Designated Activities" job-hunting visa** for a
  limited period (historically up to 1 year) to search for employment, then to a work visa once
  hired — TO VERIFY current duration and conditions.

## Dependents
- Possible under "Dependent" status for a spouse/children, subject to the sponsor's own
  status/income — TO VERIFY.

## Cost of living reference
- Highly city-dependent (Tokyo vs regional cities) — capture the specific city in the workbook
  rather than a national average.

## Notes specific to Jan/Apr 2027 intake
- Japanese academic years commonly start in **April**; many language schools also have **January
  and July** intakes — confirm which specific intake the agency post is referring to, since
  "April 2027" is the more standard/major intake for university-proper programs.

## Sources to check when verifying
- Immigration Services Agency of Japan (moj.go.jp / isa.go.jp) and JASSO (jasso.go.jp) for
  student-specific guidance.
