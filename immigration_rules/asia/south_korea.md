# South Korea — student visa/immigration checklist

> **last_verified:** 2026-09-13 (general knowledge current to Jan 2026 — re-verify TO VERIFY
> items against Korea Immigration Service / Hi Korea.)

- **Region:** Asia
- **Visa/permit name:** **D-4** (language-training) for Korean-language institutes, **D-2**
  (general study) for degree programs
- **Issuing authority:** Korea Immigration Service (Ministry of Justice), via Hi Korea; consular
  processing through the Korean Embassy covering Nepal.
- **Relevant for Nepal applicants because:** Nepal has historically been listed among
  countries subject to **stricter document/financial verification ("essential check list")**
  for the D-4 visa due to past cases of visa misuse — this materially affects processing time
  and refusal risk and is one of the most important Nepal-specific facts to keep current.
  TO VERIFY whether Nepal remains on this list and what the current requirements are.

## Entry / academic requirements
- Institution-set; degree programs (D-2) require an academic offer, language institutes (D-4)
  generally only require secondary-school completion.

## English language requirement
- Only relevant for English-taught degree tracks — institution-set, TO VERIFY.
- Most language-institute-first pathways don't require English; they build up Korean
  proficiency (TOPIK level) instead, often needed before progressing to a degree program.

## Financial / bank requirement
- Bank balance certificate (often a fixed minimum, historically cited around **KRW 10 million**
  equivalent held for a period before application) — TO VERIFY current amount and holding
  period, as this is one of the figures Korean immigration revises and enforces strictly for
  higher-risk applicant countries.

## Health insurance
- National Health Insurance enrollment is mandatory for foreign students staying beyond a
  set period — cost: TO VERIFY.

## Visa/permit process
- University/institute issues an admission/invitation document (Standard Admission Package
  process applies to certain higher-risk countries, potentially including Nepal — TO VERIFY),
  then visa application at the Korean embassy/consulate.
- Processing time and fee: TO VERIFY — can be materially longer for "essential check list"
  countries.

## Work rights while studying
- Part-time work requires separate permission and is generally more restricted for language
  institute (D-4) students than for degree (D-2) students, especially in early semesters —
  TO VERIFY current hour caps and eligibility timing (often not allowed at all in the first ~6
  months of a language course).

## Post-study work / stay-back route
- **D-10** job-seeking visa available after graduation for those seeking employment —
  TO VERIFY current duration/conditions.

## Dependents
- Generally limited for D-4 students; more feasible for D-2 (degree) students with sufficient
  standing — TO VERIFY.

## Cost of living reference
- Seoul vs regional cities differ substantially — capture the specific city.

## Notes specific to Jan/Apr 2027 intake
- Korean university academic years commonly start in **March** and **September**; language
  institutes often run **quarterly** intakes (roughly Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct) — confirm which specific
  intake an agency post's "Jan 2027"/"Apr 2027" claim actually refers to (language institute vs.
  degree program), as these are frequently conflated in marketing.
- Given the Nepal-specific extra scrutiny noted above, process-duration and rejection-risk
  claims in agency posts deserve particular skepticism/verification here.

## Sources to check when verifying
- Korea Immigration Service / Hi Korea (hikorea.go.kr), Ministry of Justice.
